Under permutation applied to both rows and columns, the diagonal stays diagonal, so the identity statistic doesn't change and its null has zero variance. Identical printed values would be odd, since a zero std should give infinities rather than matching numbers, so I suspect a coding error in how the two z computations are set up.

I'm checking why the two z-scores come out identical, and the code `test(fa,fb)` looks like it should give different values. Under a consistent permutation the diagonal is invariant, so the identity null has zero spread and should produce infinities or NaNs rather than matching numbers. Looking at the stats, both z-values depend on the far-entry mean, which shifts with the adjacency sum, so they may be tied together through that shared term.

The identity statistic is linear in the adjacency sum with a coefficient of 18/72, and the adjacency statistic moves by a factor of 1 + 18/72, so z-scoring removes the scale and the two come out identical. That makes this test degenerate: it can only tell adjacency against the rest. The adjacency results are weak, around +0.3 to +1.0 for the sums, +2.2 for 17.0 on digits, and -2.0 to -2.6 for 14.0, none of them significant. The 17.0 result leans slightly toward the adjacent-digit direction, but I'll report this test as null or weak.
